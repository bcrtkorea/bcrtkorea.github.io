About the Symposium

Control the breath, not just the margin

Respiratory motion is still one of the largest uncertainties in thoracic and upper-abdominal radiotherapy.

Tumours can move 5 to 20 mm with every breath. That motion widens margins, adds dose to the lung, liver and bowel and, in particle therapy, shifts the beam range itself. Coaching, compression, breath-hold and gating all help, but each one depends on the patient or gives something away.

Non-invasive respiratory support offers another route, which is to set the breathing pattern for the patient. CPAP raises lung volume and steadies the breath. Non-invasive mechanical ventilation (NIMV) at about 60 breaths per minute with PEEP keeps motion small, regular and reproducible, without sedation and with little patient training.

In Korea, a growing number of centres already treat patients this way, in both photon and particle therapy. This symposium brings their radiation oncologists and medical physicists together for one afternoon to share protocols, workflows and lessons from the clinic, with a live ventilator demonstration between the sessions.

SESSION I

CPAP in clinical practice

How Korean centres use continuous positive airway pressure to raise lung volume and calm the breath during treatment.

SESSION II

Building an NIMV program

Physics, workflow and team education behind bringing NIMV into photon and particle therapy.

SESSION III

NIMV-60p and beyond

Rapid, shallow ventilation at 60 breaths per minute, and its combination with deep-inspiration breath-hold.

LIVE

Hands-on demonstration

See the ventilators, masks and settings at work during a one-hour demonstration break.

For radiation oncologists, medical physicists, radiation therapists, nurses and respiratory care professionals.

Welcome Message

Bringing Korea’s breathing-control experience into one room

Dear colleagues,

It is my great pleasure to welcome you to the Symposium on Breathing Control for Radiation Therapy in Korea, held on 16 April 2027 at Yonsei Cancer Center.

Over the past few years, CPAP and non-invasive mechanical ventilation (NIMV) have moved from early experience to routine practice at several Korean centres. Rather than following ventilatory motion, they control ventilation itself.

Control is only half of the task. What has to be reproduced is the patient’s ventilatory state, not the ventilator setting, and surface-guided radiotherapy (SGRT) lets us check that state at every fraction and hold the beam when it drifts. Ventilatory control and surface guidance work best together, and both belong in this meeting.

Each team has learned how to select patients, choose settings, train staff and verify motion, often on its own. We believe now is the time to bring this experience together.

The afternoon has three sessions. The first covers clinical experience with CPAP, the second the physics and teamwork behind implementing NIMV, and the third the newest approaches built on NIMV60p and MANIV-DIBH. Between the sessions, you can see the ventilators at work in a live demonstration.

I hope this meeting becomes a place where we compare notes openly and start the collaborations that will bring controlled and verified breathing to more patients across Korea. I look forward to welcoming you in Seoul.

Jee Suk Chang
